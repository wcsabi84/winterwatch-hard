Method and interpretation

Each row provides a route identifier, the start and end locations from the supplied route file, one representative latitude and longitude, the method of selection, and a direct Google Maps link. The positions are illustrative practice estimates; the data does not establish exact measured midpoints. This distinction is part of the documented methodology, not an unreported claim of survey accuracy.

Sources: lecturer's routes-named.csv; practice coordinates.csv; manually selected Google Maps points. No exact road-chainage calculations were performed.

Method: each route is evaluated from forecast hours 18:00–06:00 (Dublin time). “Wet” means at least one hour of rain > 0 mm or dew point within 1°C of soil temperature. The minimum soil temperature is compared to the route priority threshold: P1 +1°C, P2 +0.5°C, P3 −1°C. The earliest hourly soil reading ≤ 0°C is used for finish-time calculations. One spreading run takes 1 hour per 20 km. The plan requires completion at least one hour before that reading, departures between 17:00 and 05:00, and 11-hour maximum driver duty. The forecast is modelled soil temperature (not a road sensor); snapshots and approximated GPS locations introduce uncertainty.

Resource cautions: salt is allocated to priority 1 before lower priorities, and three P1 nights’ salt is reserved at depots serving P1 routes. Available trucks/drivers are checked for overlapping runs. When no feasible departure can be calculated, the recommendation is flagged to the Duty Engineer rather than pretending a safe schedule exists. No dispatch is performed.

Part 4 — Five winters, joined

Download real Open-Meteo historical weather for ten provisional route locations and combine it with the lecturer's 7,560-row operations log. Each night runs from 18:00 through 09:00 the following morning, local Irish time. The weather archive reports soil temperature at 0–7 cm, not measured road surface temperature. ERA5-Land provides precipitation (rain plus snow), not a separate reliable rain amount.

Click Build to fetch and calculate historical weather. The first run makes up to 50 archive requests (10 routes × 5 winters) and can take a while. Finished route-winter summaries are cached in this browser.

Method: dates in ops.csv refer to the evening the night starts. Each hourly weather observation is assigned to that date when its local hour is 18–23, or to the previous date for 00–09. We calculate minimum air and 0–7 cm soil temperatures, minimum dew point, summed hourly precipitation (rain and snow) and number of hourly soil readings ≤ 0°C. The output is joined by (route_id, date). The script fails rather than silently inventing weather for unmatched nights.

Sources: Open-Meteo Historical Weather API (modelled/reanalysis, site licence and data attribution terms apply), fictional operations CSV and route descriptions. Calculated weather depends on the model, archive version, soil depth and approximate GPS points, so frost counts may differ from the lecturer's ~770 reference.
